The Phoenicians

The phoenicians were one of the first civilizations to exist. They inhabited modern-day Lebanon, North-western Syria, and North-eastern Palestine. Moreover, they stretched across the Mediterranean Sea, in places like North Africa (most notably Carthage in modern-day Tunisia) and little areas in Italy, France, Spain, Malta, Greece, and more.

What did the Phoenicians do?

  • They invented the first 22-letter abjad which eventually spread across the seas and reached Greece where the "true" alphabet was made based off of the Phoenicians invention.
  • It is believed by many that the phoenicians were the people that invented ships.
  • They had influence all over the Mediterranean due to their colonies— that served as their trading posts — all over the sea that they acquired on their boats
  • The phoenicians were the only civilization to have access to purple dye that they produced from Murex Sea Snails.
  • They built several lebanese cities such as: Tyre, Sidon, Byblos, Berytus (Beirut), Tripoli, Baalbek, and more. Moreover, they built several important cities on the coast of the mediterranean like: Carthage (Tunisia), Ibiza (Spain), Palermo (Sicily), and Malta.

The Persians

The thing that ended golden age of the Phoenicians, was the Archaemenid Empire taking over Phoenicia. However unlike most empires that would seek out to destroy culture, they bargained with the phoenicians where they had to provide a naval fleet for Persia to help them fight off the Greeks. In exhange, the Persians let them keep their kings, religion, and their trade routes. Therefore it's considered to be the "Golden Age of Diplomacy" for the Phoenicians.

What did the Persians do?

  • They set up a federal council in Tripoli, that served as a neutral meeting ground for the representatives of Tyre, Sidon, and Arwad (Syria) and the Persian satrap (governor).
  • While Tyre was famous for its fortress, the Persians prefered Sidon. There, they built the Temple of Eshmun, who was the Phoenician god of healing and renewal of life. Its structure featured a mix of Phoenician and Persian architectural styles.
  • The Phoenician kings became high-ranking admirals in the Persian military. So much so, that the King of Sidon was believed to be the second most powerful maan in the Persian naval fleet, sitting right next to the Persian Emperor Xerxes in war councils.
  • After the Persian king demanded higher taxes, the people of Sidon rebelled, but when the Persian army came to stop the rebellion; the Sidonians realized they picked a war they couldn't win, hence they locked the gates to Sidon and burned it and them at the center of it. So when Alexander the Great arrived, he easily took over every city due to most of them surrendering, EXCEPT Tyre.
  • The Persians were obsessed with the Tyrian Purple Dye that the Phoenicians had access to, so much so that when after the defeat of the Persians and Alexander the Great's conquest reached Phoenicia, he found 5000 talents (130 metric tons) of purple cloth locked away in Tyre. The incredible part? Since the dye has a unique chemical quality, it stayed vibrant purple even after being in the dark for nearly 200 years!

The Greeks

FACT: Tyre was an island. You can thank Alexander the Great for it being a peninsula now. The Greek army, led by Alexander the Great, fought the Archaemenids and eventually won and took over Modern-day Lebanon in 332 BC.

What did Alexander the Great do?

One of the greatest battles and struggles in military history, was Alexander the Great's Seige of Tyre. Tyre is made up of 2 parts: Mainland, and the heavily fortified island with massive walls protecting it. It was around 700-800 meters away from the mainland. But to Alexander the Great, no obstacle is too much. He ordered his men to use the stone and wooden remains of mainland Tyre to build a dirt bridge stretching through the sea, all the way to the island. Why did he not go by sea? Alexander the Great didn't have a navy. The Tyrians fought back brutally but desperately, they were terrified. They threw pots of boiling sand and sent fire-ships to burn his towers but they failed and Alexander breached the walls of Tyre after 7 months.

The bridge never washed away. Ocean currents deposited sand along it and accumulated until the island became fully and permanently connected to the mainland, altering the map forever.

The Hellenization— adoption of greek language and culture— of Phoenicia was inevitable after Alexander's death. His generals split his empire among themselves where they made Greek the offical language (However locals still spoke Phoenicians and Aramaic), as well as the Phoenicians started mapping them gods to Greek ones (Melqart, god of Tyre, was associated with Herakles (Heracles, god of strength), and more...)

During the seige, the Tyrians got desperate. Moreover, the phoenicians had stolen a statue of the god of the sun and music, Apollo, from the greeks prior. However, they had a dream that the god was going to fly over to Alexander and help them defeat the phoenicians. To stop the statue from doing so, the priests had chained the golden statue to the altar, so he couldn't abandon them. Obviously it didn't work, but it shows how scared they were of Alexander the Great.


The Romans

The Romans took over in 64 BC, and they didn't conquer it and move on, but made it their spiritual and agricultural center in the East of their empire. They are responsible for some of the most important historical sights and events ranging from: the Pillars of Jupiter in Baalbek, to the Crusades on Byblos and Sidon.

The Romans and Spread of their Culture

  • Agriculture: The Romans had turned the Bekaa Valley into Rome's major source of wheat which made it a pivotal landmark and an area that they depended on heavily.
  • Architecture: To show off their empire's power and prestige, they had built the largest temple complex in the entire Roman Empire in Baalbek (Heliopolis) where the Temple of Jupipter resides.
  • Additionally, the Romans integrated phoenician building habits into their routine, i-e using stones that are so massive that they defy belief. The "Trilithon" consists of 3 stones, each weighing in at about 800 tonnes. But that's not all, because nearby sits the Stone of the Pregnant Woman, which is a single stone that weighs around 1000 tonnes, and is one of the largest carved stones on Earth.
  • Education: The Romans had built a law school in Beirut, that taught the laws in Rome and attracted people from all over the Mediterranean.

Christianization

Lebanon was one of the very first to adopt christianity outside of Jerusalem. It is said that Jesus had visited Phoenicia and performed miracles there. However, since the Romans had adopted christianity as their primary and only religion, Emperor Constantine shut down all the pagan temples and destroyed parts of the Temple of Jupiter to build a Christian Church in the middle of the courtyard.

Moreover, in the 5th century, Saint Maron and his followers escaped persecution in Syria and hid in the unbreachable Qadisha Valley (known today as the Holy Valley) of Mount Lebanon. This isolated the followers of St. Maron and allowed the formation of the Maronite Catholic Church.


The Crusades

If we skip a couple hundred years, specifically to the 12th century. Islam had taken over the levant region and the arabian peninsula. In an attempt to reclaim the Holy Land, European knights marched in where they found christians in the Mountains of Lebanon known as the Maronites. They were excellent archers and scouts that aided the crusades and ultimately ended in defeat to the Muslim Mamluk Empire.
